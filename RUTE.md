# Oslo – Lindesnes – Nordkapp by bike, summer 2027

**3,590 km of riding, about 27,800 m of climbing, 39 stages, 16 ferry crossings.**
With 4 rest days this is a 6-week trip. Start in Oslo around **6–8 July** so that Rallarvegen (stage 12) falls after about 18 July, when the snow is normally gone. That puts you at Nordkapp in mid-August.

Files in this folder:

- [kart.html](kart.html): interactive map, click a stage to zoom.
- [oslo-lindesnes-nordkapp.gpx](oslo-lindesnes-nordkapp.gpx): the whole route, one track per stage.
- [stages/](stages/): one GPX per stage for the bike computer.
- [tools/](tools/): the scripts and waypoint list that made the route (`stages.py` is the editable stage list).

The track was routed with BRouter on OpenStreetMap data, so it respects the bicycle bans that are mapped. Distances exclude ferries. Climbing is smoothed and reads slightly low. "Gravel km" counts roads tagged as unpaved.

## The line

**Part A, Oslo to Lindesnes (490 km, 5 days).** The Skagerrak coast on national cycle route 1 through Drammen, Tønsberg, Larvik, Kragerø, Risør, Arendal, Kristiansand and Mandal. Easy, rolling, lots of towns. A good shakedown before the mountains.

**Part B, Lindesnes to Trondheim (1,240 km, 14 days).** Inland and high. Quiet valley roads up the Mandal river to Evje, Setesdal to Hovden, the old Haukeli road over Dyrskar, Hardangerfjord, Måbødalen up to Hardangervidda, Rallarvegen to Flåm, Aurlandsfjellet, the old King's Road over Filefjell, Tindevegen, Sognefjellet (1,434 m, the highest pass in northern Europe), Lom, Dovrefjell and down to Trondheim.

**Part C, Trondheim to Nordkapp (1,860 km, 20 days).** The coast. Fv17 Kystriksveien from Steinkjer to Bodø, ferry to Lofoten, Vesterålen, Andøya, ferry to Senja, Tromsø, the two Lyngen ferries, then the E6 to Alta and the E69 to Nordkapp.

## Landmarks and detours by stage

**On the route** needs no extra riding. **Detour** leaves the track, with the added distance or time in brackets. All of them are marked on [kart.html](kart.html).

### 1. Oslo – Tønsberg

- **Oslofjorden and Holmestrand cliffs** (on the route). The route follows the west side of the Oslofjord. Holmestrand sits under a 70 m basalt cliff.
- **Verdens Ende, Færder national park** (detour, +60 km return from Tønsberg). Smooth rock slabs and skerries at the southern tip of Tjøme, facing open Skagerrak.

### 2. Tønsberg – Kragerø

- **Mølen** (detour, +8 km return from Helgeroa). Norway's largest rolling-stone beach, part of the Ice Age end moraine and a UNESCO Geopark site.
- **Jomfruland national park** (detour, boat from Kragerø, half day). Long, flat moraine island with a pebble beach, oak forest and twin lighthouses.

### 3. Kragerø – Arendal

- **Risør skerries** (on the route). White wooden town on the open coast, surrounded by polished rock islets.
- **Lyngør** (detour, +25 km return and a short boat). Car-free island village in the outer skerries near Tvedestrand.
- **Raet national park, Hove** (detour, +25 km return from Arendal). Rolling-stone beaches and pine forest on the end moraine at Tromøy.

### 4. Arendal – Mandal

- **Blindleia** (on the route). Sheltered channel between hundreds of skerries, seen from the coast road between Lillesand and Kristiansand.
- **Sjøsanden, Mandal** (on the route). 800 m sand beach backed by pine forest, at the end of the stage.

### 5. Mandal – Lindesnes fyr

- **Lindesnes fyr** (on the route). Norway's southernmost mainland point and oldest lighthouse site. The real start of Norway end to end.
- **Spangereid canal** (on the route). Short canal across the isthmus, first dug in Viking times.

### 6. Lindesnes fyr – Evje

- **Mandalselva** (on the route). Quiet gravel roads follow the salmon river through forest and small farms all morning.
- **Syrtveitsfossen** (detour, +10 km return from Evje). Wide rapids on the Otra, a classic spot for rafting and kayaking.

### 7. Evje – Valle

- **Byglandsfjorden** (on the route). 35 km long lake that the road hugs most of the day.
- **Reiårsfossen** (on the route). A 300 m waterfall dropping beside the road at Ose.
- **Setesdal granite walls at Valle** (on the route). Huge smooth granite slabs rise straight from the valley floor.

### 8. Valle – Haukeligrend

- **Hartevatn, Hovden** (on the route). Mountain lake at 760 m where the forest gives way to open highland.
- **Byklestigen** (detour, 30 min walk). Old path cut into the cliff above the Otra, the only way up the valley before 1870.

### 9. Haukeligrend – Røldal

- **Haukelifjell and Ståvatn** (on the route). Bare high plateau with lakes at 1,000 m.
- **Dyrskar pass, 1,148 m** (on the route). The old road over the top instead of the tunnel. Snowbanks often last into July.

### 10. Røldal – Eidfjord

- **Låtefossen** (on the route). Twin 165 m waterfall that crosses under the road bridge.
- **Buerbreen** (detour, +14 km return from Odda and a 3 h hike). An arm of the Folgefonna glacier at the head of a green valley.
- **Trolltunga** (detour, +16 km return from Tyssedal and a full-day hike). The rock ledge 700 m above Ringedalsvatnet. Needs a whole extra day.
- **Sørfjorden orchards** (on the route). The quiet west side of the fjord through fruit farms under Folgefonna.
- **Husedalen waterfalls** (detour, 3–5 h hike from Kinsarvik). Four big waterfalls in a row up one valley.

### 11. Eidfjord – Haugastøl

- **Måbødalen** (on the route). The old road climbs the canyon as a car-free path with tunnels and hairpins.
- **Vøringsfossen** (on the route). 182 m waterfall with a step bridge across the gorge. One of Norway's most famous.
- **Hardangervidda** (on the route). Northern Europe's largest mountain plateau, home of wild reindeer. The road tops out at 1,250 m.
- **Kjeåsen** (detour, +20 km return from Eidfjord, 600 m climb). Mountain farm perched on a ledge above Simadalsfjorden.

### 12. Haugastøl – Flåm (Rallarvegen)

- **Finse and Hardangerjøkulen** (on the route). Highest point on the Bergen railway, under the Hardangerjøkulen ice cap.
- **Blåisen glacier tongue** (detour, 3–4 h hike from Finse). Walk to the blue ice at the edge of Hardangerjøkulen.
- **Fagernut, 1,343 m** (on the route). The high point of Rallarvegen. Snowfields and meltwater lakes.
- **Kjosfossen** (on the route). Powerful waterfall next to the Flåm railway, just below Myrdal.
- **Flåmsdalen and Rjoandefossen** (on the route). 21 hairpins down from Myrdal, then a narrow valley with waterfalls to the fjord.

### 13. Flåm – Lærdal (Aurlandsfjellet)

- **Nærøyfjorden** (detour, fjord boat Flåm–Gudvangen, half day). UNESCO World Heritage fjord, 250 m wide with 1,400 m walls.
- **Stegastein** (on the route). Viewing platform 650 m straight above Aurlandsfjorden.
- **Aurlandsfjellet, 1,306 m** (on the route). The Snow Road. Bare rock, lakes and snowbanks across the top.

### 14. Lærdal – Tyinkrysset (Kongevegen over Filefjell)

- **Lærdalselvi** (on the route). Famous salmon river along the valley floor.
- **Vindhellavegen and Borgund stave church** (on the route). Stone-built hairpins of the old King's Road, beside the best-preserved stave church in Norway.
- **Filefjell** (on the route). The historic mountain crossing between east and west, on the old road away from the E16.

### 15. Tyinkrysset – Turtagrø (Tindevegen)

- **Tyin** (on the route). Large lake at 1,080 m with the Jotunheimen peaks behind.
- **Vettisfossen** (detour, +24 km return from Øvre Årdal and a 2 h hike). 275 m free fall, the highest unregulated waterfall in northern Europe, in Utladalen.
- **Hurrungane from Tindevegen** (on the route). The toll road climbs to 1,315 m under the sharpest peaks in Norway.
- **Turtagrø** (on the route). Cradle of Norwegian mountaineering, facing Store Skagastølstind.

### 16. Turtagrø – Lom (Sognefjellet)

- **Sognefjellet, 1,434 m** (on the route). Highest mountain pass in northern Europe, with glaciers on both sides of the road.
- **Juvasshytta and Galdhøpiggen** (detour, +30 km return, 1,300 m climb). Northern Europe's highest road at 1,841 m, below Norway's highest peak. A guided glacier walk reaches the summit.
- **Bøverdalen** (on the route). Long descent through a deep green valley to Lom.

### 17. Lom – Dombås

- **Vågåvatnet** (on the route). Turquoise lake coloured by glacier meltwater from Jotunheimen.
- **Rondane national park, Mysusæter** (detour, +26 km return from Otta, 650 m climb). Norway's first national park. Rounded peaks over 2,000 m above lichen-covered plateau.
- **Kvitskriuprestene** (detour, +10 km return from Otta and a short hike). Six metre high earth pillars capped by stones.

### 18. Dombås – Berkåk (Dovrefjell)

- **Dovrefjell** (on the route). Open mountain plateau and Norway's musk ox country. Keep 200 m distance to the animals.
- **Viewpoint Snøhetta** (detour, +6 km return from Hjerkinn and a 1.5 km walk). Award-winning pavilion facing Snøhetta, 2,286 m.
- **Drivdalen** (on the route). Narrow valley down from Kongsvoll, known for its alpine flowers.
- **Magalaupet** (detour, short walk from the E6). The Driva river squeezed through a 100 m long rock slot.

### 19. Berkåk – Trondheim

- **Gauldalen** (on the route). Easy day down one of Norway's best salmon rivers, the Gaula.
- **Bymarka** (detour, on the edge of Trondheim). Forest and lakes above the city, with a view over the fjord.

### 20. Trondheim – Steinkjer

- **Trondheimsfjorden** (on the route). Farmland and fjord views all day on the old roads beside the E6.
- **Tautra** (detour, +45 km return over Frosta). Bird reserve island reached by a causeway, with monastery ruins.

### 21. Steinkjer – Namsos

- **Beitstadfjorden** (on the route). The innermost arm of Trondheimsfjorden, then forest over to Namdalen.
- **Namsen** (on the route). The 'Queen of Rivers' for salmon meets the sea at Namsos.

### 22. Namsos – Foldereid

- **Salsvatnet** (on the route). Lake only 16 m above sea level and 464 m deep, among the deepest in Europe.
- **Foldafjorden** (on the route). The first Fv17 ferry crosses this long fjord.

### 23. Foldereid – Brønnøysund

- **Bindalsfjorden and the Helgeland coast** (on the route). From here the route runs between the sea and steep granite mountains.
- **Torghatten** (detour, +30 km return from Brønnøysund and a 30 min walk). The mountain with a 160 m long hole straight through it.

### 24. Brønnøysund – Sandnessjøen

- **Vega archipelago** (detour, ferry from Horn, add a day). UNESCO World Heritage islands with eider duck houses and long flat cycling roads.
- **De syv søstre** (on the route). Seven peaks in a row, about 1,000 m high, above Sandnessjøen.

### 25. Sandnessjøen – Jektvik

- **Helgelandsbrua and Sjona** (on the route). Kilometre-long bridge, then a road that winds around the Sjona fjord.
- **Lovund** (detour, express boat from Stokkvågen, add a day). Island with one of Norway's best-known puffin colonies.
- **Arctic Circle** (on the route). Crossed on the Kilboghamn–Jektvik ferry. A globe on the shore marks the line.

### 26. Jektvik – Ørnes

- **Svartisen from Holandsfjorden** (on the route). The road runs along the fjord facing Norway's second largest glacier.
- **Engabreen** (detour, boat across the fjord and 6 km return by bike, half day). Glacier tongue that comes down almost to sea level, the lowest in mainland Europe.

### 27. Ørnes – Bodø

- **Sandhornøya beaches** (on the route). White sand and green water along Fv17 south of Bodø.
- **Saltstraumen** (on the route). The world's strongest tidal current, with whirlpools up to 10 m across. Best at mid-tide.

### 28. Moskenes – Leknes (Lofoten)

- **Å and Reine** (on the route). The classic Lofoten picture. Red fishing cabins under vertical granite peaks.
- **Reinebringen** (detour, 2 h return on stone steps). 448 m viewpoint over Reinefjorden.
- **Kvalvika** (detour, +10 km return via Fredvang and a 2 h hike). Remote sand beach between cliffs on the outer coast.
- **Ramberg beach** (on the route). Wide white beach right beside the E10.
- **Nusfjord** (detour, +12 km return). Preserved fishing village in a narrow rock inlet.

### 29. Leknes – Svolvær

- **Haukland and Uttakleiv** (detour, +20 km return from Leknes). Two of Norway's finest beaches. Uttakleiv faces the midnight sun.
- **Gimsøy** (on the route). Flat island with open sea views and marshland.
- **Henningsvær** (on the route). Fishing village spread over small islands under Vågakallen.
- **Trollfjorden** (detour, boat from Svolvær, 3 h). Fjord with a 100 m wide mouth and 1,000 m walls. Sea eagles on the way.

### 30. Svolvær – Sortland

- **Austnesfjorden** (on the route). Viewpoint over the fjord and the Sildpollnes chapel.
- **Laukvik and the outer coast** (on the route). The quiet north side of Austvågøya, open to the midnight sun.
- **Møysalen from Hadseløya** (on the route). View across to the 1,262 m peak, the highest in Vesterålen.

### 31. Sortland – Andenes

- **Andøya west coast** (on the route). National tourist route between peat bogs, white beaches and jagged peaks.
- **Måtind** (detour, 3 h hike from the road). 408 m summit with a view straight down on Høyvika beach.
- **Bleik beach and Bleiksøya** (on the route). 2 km sand beach and a cone-shaped puffin island. Boat trips run in summer.
- **Whale safari, Andenes** (detour, boat, 3–4 h). The deep-sea canyon lies close to shore here, so sperm whales are seen all summer.

### 32. Gryllefjord – Botnhamn (Senja)

- **Bergsbotn** (on the route). 44 m viewing platform over Bergsfjorden.
- **Tungeneset** (on the route). Boardwalk over the rocks facing Okshornan, the 'Devil's Jaw'.
- **Ersfjordstranda** (on the route). White beach at the head of a steep-sided fjord.
- **Segla** (detour, +24 km return to Fjordgård and a 3 h hike). The 639 m sail-shaped peak that drops vertically into Mefjorden.
- **Mefjordvær and Husøy** (detour, +10 km and +20 km return). Dead-end fishing villages on the outer coast. Husøy fills a whole small island.

### 33. Brensholmen – Tromsø

- **Sommarøy** (detour, +10 km return from Brensholmen). White coral-sand beaches and turquoise water.
- **Kvaløya** (on the route). Fjord road past small farms under 1,000 m peaks.
- **Fjellheisen, Tromsø** (detour, cable car or 1 h on the Sherpa steps). 421 m view over Tromsø and the surrounding islands.

### 34. Tromsø – Olderdalen (Lyngen)

- **Lyngen Alps** (on the route). Glaciated peaks up to 1,834 m rise straight from the fjord between the two ferries.
- **Blåisvatnet** (detour, +8 km return and a 2 h hike). Milky-blue glacier lake under the Lenangstindane peaks.
- **Lyngenfjorden** (on the route). Crossed on the Lyngseidet–Olderdalen ferry with the whole range behind you.

### 35. Olderdalen – Burfjord

- **Reisa national park** (detour, river boat from Storslett, add a day). Canyon valley with the 269 m Mollisfossen waterfall.
- **Kvænangsfjellet and Gildetun** (on the route). The old road over the 402 m pass, with a view to the Øksfjordjøkelen glacier.
- **Sørstraumen** (on the route). Strong tidal stream under the bridge. Good fishing from the shore.

### 36. Burfjord – Alta

- **Langfjorden** (on the route). The road follows the whole 30 km fjord in and out.
- **Alta rock carvings** (on the route). UNESCO World Heritage. Thousands of carvings up to 7,000 years old, on the shore at Hjemmeluft.
- **Alta canyon, Sautso** (detour, +60 km return on gravel and a 2 h hike). Northern Europe's largest canyon, up to 400 m deep.

### 37. Alta – Skaidi

- **Sennalandet** (on the route). Treeless tundra plateau at 385 m with reindeer herds all summer. Exposed to wind.
- **Repparfjordelva** (on the route). Salmon river that the road follows down to Skaidi.

### 38. Skaidi – Repvåg

- **Porsangerfjorden** (on the route). The road hugs the shore under layered slate cliffs. Reindeer on the beach are common.
- **Trollholmsund** (detour, +50 km return south from Olderfjord). White dolomite pillars on the shore, said to be trolls turned to stone.

### 39. Repvåg – Nordkapp

- **Nordkapp plateau** (on the route). The 307 m cliff at 71°10'N and the end of the road.
- **Knivskjellodden** (detour, 18 km return hike, 5–6 h). The true northernmost point of the island, 1.5 km further north than Nordkapp.
- **Kirkeporten, Skarsvåg** (detour, +6 km return and a 30 min walk). Rock arch that frames the Nordkapp horn.
- **Gjesværstappan** (detour, +42 km return to Gjesvær and a boat trip). Bird cliffs with hundreds of thousands of puffins, gannets and sea eagles.

## DNT cabins as backup

You are camping, so these are for bad weather, a mechanical or a rest. Cabins come from OpenStreetMap entries tagged as DNT or run by a local turistforening or turlag, within 5 km of the track in a straight line. The distance is not the walking distance. Anything more than about 1 km off the track is usually reached on foot.

- **Staffed lodge**: beds and meals in season, book ahead in July.
- **Self-service cabin**: beds, gas and a food store. Locked with the standard DNT key.
- **No-service cabin**: beds and a stove, bring your own food. Usually locked with the DNT key.

Get the DNT key before you leave. It needs a membership and a deposit, from a DNT office. Check each cabin on ut.no before relying on it, since some need booking and some are closed in parts of the summer.

The cabins are almost all in the mountain section, stages 8 to 18. On the coast north of Trondheim there are very few, so the backup there is a cabin at a campsite, which most campsites along Fv17, in Lofoten and on the E6 have.

## Things that decide the route

**Tunnels closed to cyclists.** These are the reason the line looks the way it does.

- **Haukelifjell and Røldal (stages 9–10).** The Haukeli, Røldal and Seljestad tunnels on the E134 are closed to bikes. The track uses the old roads over Dyrskar and Seljestadjuvet, which open when the snow is cleared, normally in June.
- **Lærdal (stages 13–15).** The Lærdal tunnel, the Fodnes tunnel and the Amla tunnel are all closed to bikes, so you cannot ride from Lærdal to Sogndal. The track instead climbs east on the old King's Road over Filefjell, drops to Øvre Årdal on Fv53 and takes Tindevegen (private toll road, free for bikes, summer only) to Turtagrø. Shortcut: a bus from Lærdal to Sogndal and then Rv55 to Turtagrø saves one day.
- **Svartisen tunnel on Fv17 (stage 26).** Closed to bikes. The track takes the Vassdalsvik–Ørnes ferry around it.
- **Kvænangsfjellet (stage 35).** The new E6 tunnels are closed to bikes. The track uses the old road over the mountain.
- **Nordkapp (stage 39).** The Nordkapp tunnel (6.9 km, under the sea, 9 % ramps) and the Honningsvåg tunnel are open to bikes. Bring strong lights and a reflective vest.

Tunnel rules change. Check each stage against Statens vegvesen's map at vegkart.no and the cycletourer.co.uk tunnel map before you go.

**Rallarvegen (stage 12).** 69 km of gravel from Haugastøl over Finse (1,343 m) to Flåm. Usually rideable from mid to late July. If snow remains between Finse and Hallingskeid, take the train for that section. The descent from Myrdal to Flåm is steep and loose.

The two summer-only ferries (Andenes–Gryllefjord and Botnhamn–Brensholmen) normally run until late August. If either is cancelled, the fallback is the E10 and E6 through Bjerkvik and Nordkjosbotn, about 2 days longer. Check the 2027 timetables when they are published.

**Traffic.** The busy stretches are the E10 in Lofoten in July, the E6 from Olderdalen to Alta, and the E6 north of Trondheim. Ride Lofoten early in the morning or in the light evening.

**Supplies.** Shops are never more than a day apart. The longest empty stretches are Alta–Skaidi–Olderfjord (stages 37–38) and Hardangervidda (stage 11). Carry a full day of food there.

**Options on the way.**

- Stage 2: the summer bike ferry Helgeroa–Langesund cuts about 25 km.
- Stage 30: the track takes the quiet north side of Austvågøya via Laukvik. The E10 through the Sløverfjord tunnel is 30 km shorter.
- Stage 32: Mefjordvær and Husøy are worthwhile dead-end detours on Senja.
- Stage 6: 38 km of gravel side roads up the Mandal valley. Fv455 and Fv42 are the paved alternative.

**Getting home.** Bus from Nordkapp or Honningsvåg to Alta, or Hurtigruten south from Honningsvåg, or fly from Honningsvåg or Alta. Book the bike space early.
